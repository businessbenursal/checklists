  Read first
  
    Myths to avoid
    
  
    Never recommend these. They’re outdated, unproven, or against Google’s rules.
    
  
    
    
      
      
    • Meta keywords tag: Google announced in 2009 that it doesn’t use it for ranking.
      • 
      
    • Keyword density targets: there’s no magic percentage. Repeating a phrase unnaturally is keyword stuffing, which Google’s spam policies name.
      • 
      
    • Minimum word counts: Google has said word count isn’t a ranking factor. Pages should be as long as the topic needs.
      • 
      
    • Star ratings from your own reviews: since 2019, Google doesn’t show review stars in search for a business’s reviews of itself, even through a widget.
      • 
      
    • FAQ schema for search snippets: Google limited FAQ rich results in 2023, and reporting in 2026 says it stopped showing them entirely.
      • 
      
    • Copy-paste city pages: Google’s spam policies treat pages targeting cities that funnel to one place as doorway pages.
      • 
      
    • Buying links or reviews: link schemes violate Google’s spam policies, and fake reviews are banned by the FTC’s 2024 rule.

    Title tags
    Does each page’s title say what it is, where, and who?
    
  
    
    
    Spot it
    
      
      
        
      • 
        
      • 
        
      • 
        
      • 
      
    0 problems found
    
    
    
      
      
    
    
  
    
  
    Why it matters
    
  
    Google says it builds the clickable title in search results mainly from the page’s title element, and that title is often the main thing people read before choosing a result. Google’s guidance asks for unique, descriptive, concise titles and specifically warns against vague titles like “Home,” repeated boilerplate, and keyword stuffing.
    
  
    
    
    Evidence
    Google recommends a unique, descriptive title for every page, with the brand added concisely.
    Evidence type: official Google Search Central documentation (“Influencing your title links”).
    
    
    Got it wrong
    Google lists vague descriptors like “Home,” identical titles across pages, and keyword-stuffed titles as common problems that lead it to rewrite the title link.
    Evidence type: same documentation.
    
  
    
  
    How to fix it
    
  
    [Service] in [City] | [Company]
    
  
      
    
    • Homepage: the main service and area plus the brand. “House Painters in Tampa, FL | Sunshine Painting.”
      • 
    
    • Each service page: its own service and area. “Cabinet Painting in Tampa | Sunshine Painting.”
      • 
    
    • Unique, front-loaded, short enough not to be cut off. Google has no official length limit.
      •

    Why it matters
    
  
    Google’s John Mueller has said headings help Google understand what each part of a page is about. He has also said there’s no special ranking boost from an H1 and that multiple H1s are handled fine. Treat headings as structure and clarity, not a trick.

    Evidence type: public statements by Google (John Mueller, 2019).
    
    
    Evidence gap
    Heading changes alone rarely move rankings much. Recommend them as part of a page rework, not as a standalone fix.
    Honest note.
    
  
    
  
    How to fix it
    
  
      
    
    • H1 = the page’s main topic in real text: service plus area.
      • 
    
    • H2s for main sections: each service, process, projects, reviews, areas served.
      • 
    
    • Style with CSS, not heading tags.

    Check 4
    Content matches what homeowners search
    Does each page use the words people actually search for, and answer what they want to know?
    
  
    
    
    Spot it
    
      
      
        
      • 
        
      • 
        
      • 
        
      • 
      
    0 problems found
    
  
    
  
    Why it matters
    
  
    Google’s SEO Starter Guide advises thinking about the words people would use to look for your content, and its helpful-content guidance asks for pages written for people with first-hand expertise. Its spam policies name keyword stuffing, including blocks of city names, as a violation.
    
  
    
    
    Evidence
    Google recommends people-first content that uses the words searchers use and shows real expertise.
    Evidence type: official Google Search Central guidance (SEO Starter Guide; helpful content).
    
    
    Got it wrong
    Google’s spam policies list keyword stuffing, such as repeating words unnaturally or listing cities a page is trying to rank for, as a violation.
    Evidence type: official Google spam policies.
    
  
    
  
    Testing for keyword cannibalization. Outside: search site:theirdomain.com cabinet painting (swap in each main service) and see whether several similar pages come up. With access: in Search Console’s Performance report, click a main query and open the Pages tab; several URLs sharing impressions for it is the clearest sign. Honest note: Google’s John Mueller has said this isn’t a penalty, and several pages appearing for a topic isn’t always a problem. It matters when similar pages split attention and the wrong one shows up, like an old blog post instead of the service page.
    
  
    How to fix it
    
  
      
    
    • Find the real phrases: type “[service] [city]” into Google and note the autocomplete suggestions and “People also ask” questions.
      • 
    
    • Fix cannibalization: choose the one page that should rank for each service-and-area target. Merge weaker duplicates into it with 301 redirects, or rewrite them to cover a clearly different topic, and link from related pages to the main one with descriptive text.
      • 
    
    • Answer the real questions on each page: process, timeline, prep, cost factors, photos of that work.
      • 
    
    • Write naturally; mention the area where it fits, not in every sentence.
      •

    Why it matters
    
  
    Google’s link guidance says it uses links to find pages and uses the link text to understand what the linked page is about. Links need to be real HTML links (an a element with an href) for Google to follow them reliably, and the link text should be descriptive.
    
  
    
    
    Evidence
    Google recommends descriptive, concise anchor text that’s relevant to the page it links to.
    Evidence type: official Google Search Central documentation (link best practices).
    
    
    Got it wrong
    Google notes that it can’t reliably follow links that aren’t real HTML links, such as elements that only navigate through JavaScript.
    Evidence type: same documentation.
    
  
    
  
    How to fix it
    
  
      
    
    • Link every service page from the menu and the homepage.
      • 
    
    • Use descriptive link text: “see our cabinet painting work,” not “click here.”
      • 
    
    • Connect related pages: service ↔ city ↔ project write-ups.
      • 
    
    • Use real links for navigation.

    Spot it

      •

    0 problems found
    
  
    
  
    Why it matters
    
  
    Structured data labels the business details (name, phone, hours, area served) so search engines read them reliably. Schema.org has a specific HousePainter type. Google documents local business markup, but says adding it doesn’t guarantee any search feature.
    
  
    
    
    Evidence

    Evidence type: official Google Search Central documentation.

      •

      • 
    
    • Don’t promise review stars from their own reviews; that isn’t possible in Google.

    Why it matters
    
  
    Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance, and prominence, and categories are a core relevance signal. Google’s guidelines say to choose the fewest categories it takes to describe the core business, to keep one profile per business, and to use the business’s real-world name.

      •

      • 
    
    • Business name: exactly as on their signage and branding.
      •

    Check 13
    Reviews
    How do their reviews compare, and are they collected within the rules?
    
  
    
    
    Spot it
    
      
      
        
      • 
        
      • 
        
      • 
        
      • 
      
    0 problems found
    
  
    
  
    Why it matters
    
  
    Google says more reviews and positive ratings can improve a business’s local ranking, and recommends replying to reviews. Its review policy allows businesses to ask for genuine reviews but bans incentives, discouraging negative reviews, and selectively asking only happy customers. An April 2026 update also bars asking staff to hit review quotas or to get reviews that mention specific content, such as staff names.
    
  
    
    
    Evidence
    Google lists reviews and owner replies among the things that help local ranking.
    Evidence type: official Google Business Profile Help.
    
    
    Got it wrong
    Reviews tied to incentives or selective solicitation violate Google’s Maps content policy and can be removed. Fake or bought reviews are also banned under the FTC’s 2024 rule.
    Evidence type: Google Maps user-contributed content policy; FTC regulation.
    
  
    
  
    How to fix it
    
  
      
    
    • Ask every customer after the job, with a text containing a direct review link. No incentives, and ask everyone, not just happy customers.
      • 
    
    • Reply to every review, briefly and personally, including negative ones.
      • 
    
    • Don’t set staff quotas or ask for reviews mentioning specific people or words.
      •

    Check 19
    HTTPS, redirects, and duplicates
    Does every version of the site lead to one secure address?
    
  
    Spot it
    
    
      
      
      • 
      
      • 
      
      • 
      
      • 
    
    0 problems found
    
  
    Why it matters
    
  
    Google announced HTTPS as a ranking signal in 2014, and browsers label non-HTTPS pages “Not secure.” Several working versions of the same site split signals and can confuse which page Google shows; permanent redirects and canonical tags tell Google which one is the main version.
    
  
    
    
    Evidence
    Google recommends permanent (301) redirects when URLs change, and canonical tags to indicate the preferred version of duplicate pages.
    Evidence type: official Google Search Central documentation.
    
    
    Got it wrong
    Redesigns that change URLs without redirects are a common cause of lost search traffic, because old links and rankings point to pages that no longer exist.
    Evidence type: Google’s site-move guidance.
    
  
    
  
    How to fix it
    
  
      
    
    • Force HTTPS and pick one host (www or not); redirect everything else with 301s.
      • 
    
    • Redirect old URLs to their closest current page.
      • 
    
    • Add canonical tags where duplicates must exist.
      •

    0 problems found
    
  
    Why it matters
    
  
    Core Web Vitals are Google’s measures of loading, responsiveness, and visual stability, with “good” thresholds of about 2.5 seconds, 200 milliseconds, and 0.1. Google treats page experience as one signal among many, and relevance still matters more. The bigger effect is on visitors: in Vodafone’s A/B test, a 31% faster main image brought 8% more sales.
    
  
    
    
    Got it right
    Vodafone’s optimized landing page (5.7 s vs 8.3 s to show the main content) produced 8% more sales and a 15% better lead-to-visit rate.
    Evidence type: company A/B test on Google’s web.dev (2021).
    
    
    Evidence gap
    Speed fixes rarely move rankings by themselves. Present them as keeping visitors, not as a ranking promise.
    Honest note.
    
  
    
  
    How to fix it
    
  
      
    
    • Compress and resize images; use modern formats and set width and height.
      • 
    
    • Replace sliders and autoplay video at the top with one optimized image.
      • 
    
    • Delay or remove non-essential scripts and unused plugins.
      • 
    
    • Use good hosting with caching.

    Check 22
    Site structure and click depth
    Are the money pages close to the homepage and easy to reach?
    
  
    Spot it
    
    
      
      
      • 
      
      • 
      
      • 
    
    0 problems found
    
  
    Why it matters
    
  
    Google finds pages through links, and Google’s John Mueller has said that how easily a page can be reached from the homepage signals how important it is. Visitors benefit the same way: the fewer clicks to the right service page, the fewer people give up.
    
  
    
    
    Evidence
    Google’s link guidance explains that it discovers and understands pages through links from other pages.
    Evidence type: official Google documentation; public statements by Google (John Mueller).

    Honest note.
    
  
    
  
    How to fix it
    
  
      
    
    • Link every main service from the menu and homepage.
      • 
    
    • Link projects and guides from the matching service pages.
      • 
    
    • Give orphan pages links, or remove them if they aren’t useful.

    Service page depth

    Spot it
    
    
      
      
      • 
      
      • 
      
      • 
    
    0 problems found
    
  
    Why it matters
    
  
    Each service is its own search and its own decision. Google’s helpful-content guidance asks whether a page leaves people feeling they learned enough to reach their goal, and whether it shows first-hand expertise. NN/g found people judge whether a company serves “people like me” by whether the site shows that kind of work.

    • Write each page from scratch; no shared boilerplate.

    Why it matters

    Google profile performance

    Spot it

    Why it matters
    
  
    For many painters, the Google profile brings more calls than the website. Google’s profile performance reports show calls, website clicks, and direction requests.     Check 29
    Lead tracking
    Can the owner see which leads came from search, and which became jobs?
    
  
    Spot it
    
    
      
      
      • 
      
      • 
      
      • 
      
      • 
    
    0 problems found
    
  
    Why it matters
    
  
    SEO only matters if it brings estimate requests. GA4 can record form submissions and phone taps as key events, and a simple lead log by source closes the loop from search to booked job. This is how you prove results to a client.
    
  
    
    
    Evidence
    Google’s GA4 documentation describes marking actions like form submissions as key events.
