  Read first
  
    How evidence is labeled
    
  
    
    
      
      
    • Research: studies with real users or large data (NN/g, Baymard, Spiegel Research Center). Strongest.
      • 
      
    • Practitioner test: a real test or case study published by the people who ran it (Copyhackers, CXL). Valuable, but one business’s result.
      • 
      
    • Framework: a respected method, not proof (April Dunford’s positioning, StoryBrand).
      • 
      
    • Regulation: rules that apply regardless of results (FTC).
      • 
    
    
  
    

    



    
  
    Check 1
    Ideal customer
    Is it clear who this company serves best, and does the site speak to them?
    
  
    Spot it
    
    
      
      
      • 
      
      • 
      
      • 
    
    0 problems found
    
    
    Ask the owner
    • Which five recent jobs would you happily repeat? What do those customers have in common (home type, area, budget)?
    • Which jobs do you want fewer of?
    • Who refers you the most work?
    
  
    Why it matters
    
  
    NN/g found people look for signs a company serves “people like me.” Cleaning services that only showed large condos and offices made participants with smaller homes question whether they’d be welcome; as NN/g put it, no one wants to feel like a second-rate customer. April Dunford’s positioning framework starts from the same idea: define the customers who care most about what you do best.
    
  
    
    
    Research
    NN/g observed people preferring service sites whose photos and content matched their own situation.
    Evidence type: research (NN/g usability study, 2016).
    
    
    Framework
    Dunford’s Obviously Awesome defines target customers as those who care a lot about the value only you can deliver.
    Evidence type: framework.
    
  
    
  
    How to fix it
    
  
      
    
    • Name the best-fit customer in the brief: home type, area, job size, what they care about.
      • 
    
    • Make the hero, gallery, and reviews reflect them.
      • 
    
    • Give secondary audiences their own path (for example, a page for property managers).
      • 
  
    

    



    
  
    Check 2
    Competitor messaging
    What do the local alternatives claim, and where’s the open space?
    
  
    
    
    Spot it
    
      
      
        
      • 
        
      • 
        
      • 
      
    0 problems found
    
    
    
      
      
    
    
  
    
  
    Why it matters
    
  
    Positioning is relative. Dunford’s framework begins with competitive alternatives: what customers would do if this company didn’t exist, which includes doing nothing or DIY. NN/g found that when offerings look similar, people fall back on surface cues like design, so standing apart needs a real, visible difference.
    
  
    
    
    Framework
    Dunford’s process lists competitive alternatives first, then the unique attributes that only this company has.
    Evidence type: framework (Obviously Awesome, 2019).
    
    
    Research
    In NN/g’s comparison study, when two services were similar, most participants chose based on which site looked more appealing.
    Evidence type: research (NN/g, 2016), indirect.
    
  
    
  
    How to do it: search “[service] [city]” and take the top 3 map results. Copy each one’s hero headline, subheadline, and 3 main claims into a table, then add this company. Circle claims everyone makes, and look for true claims nobody makes.
    
  
    How to fix it
    
  
      
    
    • Drop claims everyone makes from the headline (“quality,” “fair prices”).
      • 
    
    • Lead with a true claim competitors don’t make, backed by proof.
      • 
    
    • Address the real alternatives, including waiting or DIY.
      • 
  
    

    



    
  
    Check 3
    Differentiation
    What’s genuinely different about this company, and can they prove it?
    
  
    Spot it
    
    
      
      
      • 
      
      • 
      
      • 
    
    0 problems found
    
    
    Ask the owner
    • What do you do that most painters around here skip?
    • Why do customers say they chose you over the other quotes?
    • What can you prove: numbers, warranty terms, certifications?
    
  
    Why it matters
    
  
    Dunford’s framework ties each unique attribute to the value it creates for customers and to proof. Copyhackers’ voice-of-customer method adds a practical source: reviews often state the real differentiator in the customer’s own words. In one well-known Copyhackers example for a rehab center, the winning headline was taken almost word for word from a customer review.
    
  
    
    
    Practitioner test
    Copyhackers’ review mining for a rehab center produced the headline “If you think you need rehab, you do,” lifted from a real review. Copyhackers reports its copy changes for that client raised conversions by 26%.
    Evidence type: practitioner case study (Copyhackers). One business, reported by the firm that did the work.
    
    
    Framework
    Dunford warns that attributes only matter when tied to value customers care about; a difference nobody values isn’t positioning.
    Evidence type: framework.
    
  
    
  
    How to fix it
    
  
      
    
    • Mine reviews: read 20–30 of their Google reviews and tally what gets praised most.
      • 
    
    • Pick 1–3 differences that customers value and the company can prove.
      • 
    
    • Record each with its proof in the brief.
      • 
  
    

    



    
  
    Check 4
    Value proposition
    Can the company’s reason to choose them be said in one clear sentence?
    
  
    Spot it
    
    
      
      
      • 
      
      • 
      
      • 
    
    0 problems found
    
  
    Why it matters
    
  
    NN/g’s summary of Microsoft Research data found that the first 10 seconds decide whether visitors stay, and recommends communicating the value proposition within that window. Copyhackers recommends building it from customer language rather than inventing it.
    
  
    
    
    Research
    Visitors who get past the first 10 seconds tend to stay much longer.
    Evidence type: research (Liu, White, and Dumais, 2010, via NN/g).
    
    
    Evidence gap
    No reliable public study measures value propositions for painters specifically. Present it as clarity, not a guaranteed lift.
    Honest note.
    
  
    
  
    How to fix it
    
  
    For [ideal customer] who want [outcome], we [what we do]. Unlike [alternative], we [differentiator].
    
  
      
    
    • Write it in the brief first, then turn it into the hero headline and subheadline.
      • 
    
    • Use customer phrases from reviews where possible.
      • 
  
    

    



    
  
    Check 5
    Proof inventory
    What proof does the company have, and is it being used?
    
  
    Spot it
    
    
      
      
      • 
      
      • 
      
      • 
    
    0 problems found
    
    
    Ask the owner
    • How many homes have you painted? Since when?
    • What warranty do you put in writing?
    • Any awards, certifications, or memberships you can show?
    
  
    Why it matters
    
  
    Northwestern’s Spiegel Research Center found purchase likelihood for a product with five reviews was 270% higher than for one with none, with a bigger effect for higher-priced items. The FTC expects a reasonable basis for objective claims, and its 2024 rule bans fake reviews.
    
  
    
    
    Research
    Reviews raised purchase likelihood sharply in Spiegel’s data, especially early on.
    Evidence type: research (Spiegel Research Center, 2017; online retail data).
    
    
    Regulation
    Unsupported objective claims and fake reviews create legal risk under FTC rules.
    Evidence type: regulation (FTC).
    
  
    
  
    How to fix it
    
  
      
    
    • List every piece of proof in the brief, next to the claim it supports.
      • 
    
    • Put the strongest proof near the top of key pages.
      • 
    
    • Replace unsupported superlatives with numbers.
      • 
  
    

    



    
  
    Check 6
    Offer and risk reversal
    Is the first step easy, and does hiring them feel safe?
    
  
    Spot it
    
    
      
      
      • 
      
      • 
      
      • 
    
    0 problems found
    
    
    Ask the owner
    • What’s the easiest way for someone to start: a visit, photos by text, a video call?
    • What do you guarantee, in writing?
    • When does your schedule genuinely fill up?
    
  
    Why it matters
    
  
    NN/g found people value companies that are upfront about guarantees. In 37signals’ Highrise headline test, leading with a free, low-risk first step produced 30% more signups. The FTC lists fake countdown timers and false scarcity as deceptive design.
    
  
    
    
    Practitioner test
    Highrise’s winning headline, “30-day Free Trial on All Accounts,” beat the original by 30%.
    Evidence type: practitioner test (37signals, 2009). Software, not a painter.
    
    
    Regulation
    The FTC’s 2022 dark patterns report names baseless countdown timers and false scarcity claims.
    Evidence type: regulation (FTC staff report).
    
  
    
  
    How to fix it
    
  
      
    
    • Offer one easy first step that the company genuinely provides.
      • 
    
    • State guarantee terms specifically, and only what they honor.
      • 
    
    • Use real timing only.
      • 
  
    

    



    
  
    Check 7
    Price position
    Does the site attract the price level the company actually charges?
    
  
    Spot it
    
    
      
      
      • 
      
      • 
      
      • 
    
    0 problems found
    
    
    Ask the owner
    • What’s your average job size?
    • How often do you lose bids on price?
    • Which leads are a bad fit, and why?
    
  
    Why it matters
    
  
    A mismatch wastes estimates: premium companies get bargain hunters, and budget companies scare off their buyers. NN/g found people rule out service companies that hide basic cost information, and accept ranges if the cost factors are clear.
    
  
    
    
    Research
    NN/g recommends giving ranges and cost factors rather than hiding prices behind long forms.
    Evidence type: research (NN/g, 2016).
    
    
    Evidence gap
    There’s no reliable public research on price signaling for painting contractors. Use the owner’s own lead data as the evidence.
    Honest note.
    
  
    
  
    How to fix it
    
  
      
    
    • Record the price position in the brief and match tone, photos, and proof to it.
      • 
    
    • Explain what drives price, and why their price is worth it (prep, materials, warranty).
      • 
  
    

    



    
  
    Check 8
    Consistency across channels
    Does the company say the same thing everywhere customers meet it?
    
  
    Spot it
    
    
      
      
      • 
      
      • 
      
      • 
    
    0 problems found
    
    
    Ask the owner
    • Can you send photos of your trucks, yard signs, and an estimate document?
    
  
    Why it matters
    
  
    Consistency is one of Nielsen’s core usability heuristics: people shouldn’t have to wonder whether different words mean the same thing. When the site, profile, and signs tell different stories, the message is weaker and harder to remember.
    
  
    
    
    Research
    The heuristic “consistency and standards” is based on decades of usability practice.
    Evidence type: established usability heuristic (Nielsen, NN/g).
    
    
    Evidence gap
    No reliable public study measures cross-channel message consistency for local contractors. Present it as clarity and memorability.
    Honest note.
    
  
    
  
    How to fix it
    
  
      
    
    • Use the brief as the single source for the one-liner, offer, and call to action.
      • 
    
    • Update the profile description, social bios, and print materials to match.
      • 
  
    

    



    
  
    Check 9
    Homepage hero copy
    Do the headline, subheadline, and button say who, what, where, and what to do next?
    
  
    Spot it
    
    
      
      
      • 
      
      • 
      
      • 
      
      • 
    
    0 problems found
    
  
    Why it matters
    
  
    NN/g’s summary of Microsoft Research data found the first 10 seconds decide whether visitors stay. In 37signals’ Highrise test, the headline that led with what the customer gets beat the original by 30%, while the headline naming the company’s action did worst.
    
  
    
    
    Practitioner test
    Highrise’s “30-day Free Trial on All Accounts” with “Sign-up takes less than 60 seconds” produced 30% more signups than the original.
    Evidence type: practitioner test (37signals, 2009). Software, not a painter.
    
    
    Practitioner test
    The original “Start a Highrise Account” performed worst of five, naming what the company wanted rather than what the customer gets.
    Evidence type: same test.
    
  
    
  
    How to fix it
    
  
    Headline: [Service] in [area] + [outcome or difference] · Subheadline: proof or risk reversal · Button: what they get
    
  
      
    
    • Turn the brief’s value proposition into the headline and subheadline.
      • 
    
    • Button: first person and specific, like “Get my free estimate.”
      • 
  
    

    



    
  
    Check 10
    Message flow
    Does the homepage move from the homeowner’s problem to a plan to success?
    
  
    Spot it
    
    
      
      
      • 
      
      • 
      
      • 
      
      • 
    
    0 problems found
    
  
    Why it matters
    
  
    StoryBrand (Donald Miller) frames the customer as the hero with a problem, the business as the guide with a plan, and the page as leading to a call to action and a picture of success. It’s a widely used framework, not research, but it lines up with what research shows: people decide fast and respond to content about their own situation.
    
  
    
    
    Framework
    StoryBrand’s sequence: character, problem, guide, plan, call to action, avoid failure, success.
    Evidence type: framework (Building a StoryBrand, 2017).
    
    
    Evidence gap
    StoryBrand’s published results are its own case studies, not independent research. Don’t cite it as proven.
    Honest note.
    
  
    
  
    How to fix it
    
  
    Problem → Guide (empathy + authority) → 3-step plan → Call to action → Success
    
  
      
    
    • Order the homepage sections to follow that flow, ending with the call to action.
      • 
    
    • Move history to the About page.
      • 
  
    

    



    
  
    Check 11
    Customer language
    Does the copy use the words customers actually use in their reviews?
    
  
    Spot it
    
    
      
      
      • 
      
      • 
      
      • 
    
    0 problems found
    
  
    Why it matters
    
  
    Copyhackers’ voice-of-customer method treats research as the biggest part of copywriting: mine reviews for the exact words people use about their problems and results, then build copy from them. Their rehab-center headline, taken almost word for word from a customer review, is the classic example.
    
  
    
    
    Practitioner test
    Copyhackers reports a 26% conversion lift from its copy work for a rehab center, where the headline came from review mining.
    Evidence type: practitioner case study (Copyhackers).
    
    
    Research
    In Morkes and Nielsen’s study, promotional “marketese” scored lowest; plain, objective language improved measured usability by 27%.
    Evidence type: research (Morkes and Nielsen, 1997).
    
  
    
  
    How to fix it
    
  
      
    
    • Mine 20–30 reviews (theirs and competitors’) and copy recurring phrases into the brief.
      • 
    
    • Use those phrases in headlines, objection answers, and service pages.
      • 
    
    • Never quote a review as if it’s the company’s claim unless it’s attributed.
      • 
  
    

    



    
  
    Check 12
    Benefits over features
    Does every feature come with what it means for the homeowner?
    
  
    Spot it
    
    
      
      
      • 
      
      • 
    
    0 problems found
    
  
    Why it matters
    
  
    NN/g estimates people read at most about 28% of the words on an average page, so the benefit has to be stated outright. Highrise’s winning subheadline turned a feature into a benefit: “Sign-up takes less than 60 seconds.”
    
  
    
    
    Research
    Nielsen’s analysis found people read only a fraction of page text, so meaning must be explicit.
    Evidence type: research (Nielsen, 2008).
    
    
    Evidence gap
    No reliable public study compares benefit-led and feature-led copy for painters. Present it as clarity.
    Honest note.
    
  
    
  
    How to fix it
    
  
    [Benefit], because [feature as proof]
    
  
    • Run the “so what?” test on every claim until it reaches something the homeowner feels.
    

    



    
  
    Check 13
    Specificity and proof
    Are claims concrete, and is proof right next to them?
    
  
    Spot it
    
    
      
      
      • 
      
      • 
      
      • 
    
    0 problems found
    
  
    Why it matters
    
  
    Objective, specific writing outperformed promotional writing in Morkes and Nielsen’s study, because promotional claims make people stop and question them. The FTC also expects a reasonable basis for objective advertising claims.
    
  
    
    
    Research
    The objective version of the test site improved measured usability by 27% over the promotional one.
    Evidence type: research (Morkes and Nielsen, 1997).
    
    
    Regulation
    Objective claims need substantiation under the FTC’s advertising policy.
    Evidence type: regulation (FTC, 1983).
    
  
    
  
    How to fix it
    
  
      
    
    • Swap superlatives for facts from the brief’s proof list.
      • 
    
    • Place proof beside each claim: a review next to “spotless cleanup.”
      • 
  
    

    



    
  
    Check 14
    Objection handling
    Does the copy answer the worries that stop homeowners from calling?
    
  
    Spot it
    
    
      
      
      • 
      
      • 
      
      • 
    
    0 problems found
    
    
    Ask the owner
    • What do people worry about most on the first call?
    • Why do people say no after an estimate?
    
  
    Why it matters
    
  
    NN/g found people hiring in-home services want to know whom they’ll deal with and how the service works, and that sites hiding basic information were ruled out quickly. Copyhackers’ method treats objections from reviews and sales calls as core copy material.
    
  
    
    
    Research
    Participants valued practical, upfront information and visited FAQ pages to find it.
    Evidence type: research (NN/g, 2016).
    
    
    Research
    A participant ruled out a cleaning company in 35 seconds because it didn’t state its rates.
    Evidence type: research (NN/g, 2016).
    
  
    
  
    How to fix it
    
  
      
    
    • List objections and answers in the brief.
      • 
    
    • Answer them in a “What to expect” section and on service pages, in short, specific lines.
      • 
  
    

    



    
  
    Check 15
    Service page copy structure
    Does each service page follow a clear, scannable structure?
    
  
    Spot it
    
    
      
      
      • 
      
      • 
      
      • 
    
    0 problems found
    
  
    Why it matters
    
  
    In Morkes and Nielsen’s study, a scannable version of a site scored 47% higher in measured usability, and combining concise, scannable, and objective writing scored 124% higher. Google’s helpful-content guidance also favors pages that fully answer the visitor’s question.
    
  
    
    
    Research
    Scannable layout alone improved measured usability by 47%.
    Evidence type: research (Morkes and Nielsen, 1997).
    
    
    Research
    79% of test users scanned pages; only 16% read word by word.
    Evidence type: same study.
    
  
    
  
    How to fix it
    
  
    Headline → Outcome + proof → What’s included → Process → Photos → FAQs → Reviews → Call to action
    
  
    • Write each page from scratch, with subheadings and short paragraphs.
    

    



    
  
    Check 16
    About page story
    Does the About page introduce real people and a real story?
    
  
    Spot it
    
    
      
      
      • 
      
      • 
      
      • 
    
    0 problems found
    
    
    Ask the owner
    • Why did you start the company?
    • What’s something you do on every job that customers don’t see?
    
  
    Why it matters
    
  
    NN/g found that in high-trust industries where you invite strangers into your home, people want a better understanding of whom they’ll do business with, and they preferred services showing the actual people doing the work.
    
  
    
    
    Research
    Participants preferred services whose photos showed real staff.
    Evidence type: research (NN/g, 2016).
    
    
    Research
    Generic photos were more likely to be dismissed as filler.
    Evidence type: same study.
    
  
    
  
    How to fix it
    
  
    • Owner name, photo, short story, crew photos, and how long they’ve served the area. Keep the customer as the hero; the company is the guide.
    

    



    
  
    Check 17
    Calls to action and microcopy
    Do buttons, form labels, and confirmation messages make the next step clear and easy?
    
  
    Spot it
    
    
      
      
      • 
      
      • 
      
      • 
      
      • 
    
    0 problems found
    
  
    Why it matters
    
  
    On an Unbounce page, changing “Start your free 30 day trial” to “Start my free 30 day trial” raised click-through by 90%. Baymard’s form research found split name fields confused many users. Nielsen’s consistency heuristic says one action should have one name.
    
  
    
    
    Practitioner test
    The first-person button wording won by 90% in a three-week test.
    Evidence type: practitioner test (Michael Aagaard, Unbounce). One page; results vary.
    
    
    Research
    42% of Baymard’s test participants typed their full name into a “First Name” field at least once.
    Evidence type: research (Baymard Institute).
    
  
    
  
    How to fix it
    
  
      
    
    • Buttons: action plus what they get, in first person, the same everywhere.
      • 
    
    • Labels above fields; mark optional ones.
      • 
    
    • Under the button: what happens next and how fast, if true.
      • 
  
    

    



    
  
    Check 18
    Readability
    Is the copy easy for any homeowner to read quickly?
    
  
    Spot it
    
    
      
      
      • 
      
      • 
      
      • 
    
    0 problems found
    
  
    Why it matters
    
  
    NN/g’s research on plain language finds that even highly educated readers prefer clear, simple text because it’s faster to read. US plain-language guidance (plainlanguage.gov) recommends short sentences, common words, and active voice.
    
  
    
    
    Research
    NN/g found plain language helps all readers, including experts, scan and understand faster.
    Evidence type: research (NN/g, “Plain Language Is for Everyone, Even Experts”).
    
    
    Evidence gap
    Readability scores are rough guides, not proof. Use them to spot problems, not as targets.
    Honest note.
    
  
    
  
    How to fix it
    
  
      
    
    • Short sentences, common words, active voice.
      • 
    
    • Check with a free readability tool (such as Hemingway Editor) and fix the worst sentences.
      • 
  
    

    



    
  
    Check 19
    Testimonial selection
    Are the right reviews shown in the right places?
    
  
    Spot it
    
    
      
      
      • 
      
      • 
      
      • 
      
      • 
    
    0 problems found
    
  
    Why it matters
    
  
    Spiegel Research Center found purchase likelihood peaked at ratings of 4.0–4.7 rather than a perfect 5.0, which suggests people distrust reviews that look too perfect. NN/g found people trust external review sites more than testimonials on a company’s own site, so linking to the source helps.
    
  
    
    
    Research
    Purchase likelihood peaked at 4.0–4.7 stars in Spiegel’s data.
    Evidence type: research (Spiegel Research Center, 2017).
    
    
    Research
    On-site testimonials alone were viewed with more skepticism than external reviews.
    Evidence type: research (NN/g, 2016).
    
  
    
  
    How to fix it
    
  
      
    
    • Choose specific reviews with a name, area, service, and detail.
      • 
    
    • Match reviews to pages and objections.
      • 
    
    • Link to the Google reviews. Never edit a review’s meaning.
      • 
  
    

    



    
  
    Check 20
    Google profile copy
    Do the profile description and updates carry the same message, within Google’s rules?
    
  
    Spot it
    
    
      
      
      • 
      
      • 
      
      • 
    
    0 problems found
    
  
    Why it matters
    
  
    For local searches, the profile is often the first thing a homeowner reads. Google’s guidelines say the business description should describe the business, without links or a focus on promotions and prices.
    
  
    
    
    Evidence
    Google’s guidance asks for an accurate description of the business and its services.
    Evidence type: official Google Business Profile guidelines.
    
    
    Evidence gap
    Google doesn’t say the description affects ranking. Treat it as copy for customers.
    Honest note.
    
  
    
  
    How to fix it
    
  
      
    
    • Rewrite the description from the brief: who, what, where, difference, proof.
      • 
    
    • Use updates for recent projects and real offers, in the same voice.
